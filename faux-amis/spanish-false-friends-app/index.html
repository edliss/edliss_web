Faux Amis

Spanish False Friends App for iPhone

A focused way to practise the Spanish words that look reassuringly English until they mean something else.

Spanish can feel generous to an English speaker. Many words are recognisable before you have formally learned them. That overlap is useful, but it also creates a particular kind of mistake: the word looks familiar, so you stop checking.

False friends are pairs that look or sound alike across two languages but do not share the meaning you expect. Some are memorable enough to become classroom jokes. Others are quiet errors that can remain in your vocabulary for years.

A few common Spanish examples make the problem clear:

  • embarazada means “pregnant,” not “embarrassed.”
  • éxito means “success,” not “exit.” An exit is una salida.
  • una carpeta is a folder, not a carpet.
  • largo usually means “long,” not “large.”

The difficulty is not simply remembering four corrections. In real reading or conversation, the false meaning arrives first because it is visually close to English. You need enough practice for the Spanish meaning to become the automatic one.

Short examples are more useful than isolated pairs. They show whether a word refers to a person, an object, a result or a measurement, and they make the tempting English interpretation visibly awkward. That small amount of context helps the correction last without turning each word into a full lesson.

Practice the direction you need

Faux Amis treats the two directions separately. Spanish to English helps when you are reading or listening and need to recognise the real meaning. English to Spanish makes you retrieve the Spanish word from a meaning you already know. The prompt, answer and explanation all change with the selected direction.

Each question is multiple choice. After you answer, the app shows the correct meaning beside the misleading one, followed by example sentences and translations. You can hear the word through on-device speech synthesis. Nothing depends on a streamed lesson or a permanent connection.

The words are divided into Common, Medium, Advanced and Partial tiers. Partial false friends deserve their own tier because their meanings overlap in some contexts but not others. Treating those words as completely unrelated would be just as misleading as treating them as perfect translations.

A language app without another subscription

I prefer small, focused software that can be bought once and kept. Faux Amis is a one-time purchase. There is no subscription, no in-app purchase and no free trial that turns into a recurring charge. The price shown by the App Store depends on your storefront, so the website does not publish a number that can go stale.

The word data is included with the app, which means quizzes work fully offline. Progress by tier, accuracy, session history and a daily study streak are stored for you. Optional reminders can help with regular practice.

There is no separate account or sign-up. The app does not include tracking or analytics. If you use more than one device, your progress can sync privately through iCloud.

The aim is deliberately narrow. Faux Amis is not a complete Spanish course and does not pretend to be one. It is a way to practise one recurring source of errors until the familiar-looking word stops sending you toward the wrong meaning.
