Italian False Friends for English Speakers

Italian often looks readable before it feels readable. False friends are one reason the first impression can be wrong.

English speakers meet plenty of Italian words that seem understandable at sight. The spelling is close, the sentence looks ordinary, and the guessed meaning appears to fit. Then a room becomes a camera or a factory becomes fabric.

These misleading pairs are false friends. They are not random mistakes. The words may share older roots or simply resemble one another, but their everyday meanings have moved apart. The resemblance is strong enough to encourage a quick answer and weak enough to make that answer wrong.

Here are a few useful Italian false friends for English speakers:

  • una camera is a room, often a bedroom, not a camera.
  • i parenti are relatives, not specifically parents. Parents are i genitori.
  • il burro is butter, not a donkey.
  • una fabbrica is a factory, not fabric.
  • una libreria can be a bookshop or a bookcase; a public library is una biblioteca.

Some traps are easy to remember once they have produced a surprising image. Others are harder because the wrong meaning is plausible. A hotel has rooms and cameras. A city can have factories and fabric shops. Context eventually corrects the mistake, but it may do so only after you have misunderstood the sentence.

The best clue is often the rest of the sentence. A verb, article or nearby noun can rule out the English lookalike. Practising whole examples makes those clues easier to notice and gives the correct Italian meaning a natural setting instead of leaving it as another item in a vocabulary table.

Turn recognition into recall

A list is a useful reference, but it does not recreate the moment when you must choose a meaning. False Friends uses short multiple-choice quizzes for that step. Each question starts with a word card. Once you answer, you see the correct meaning, the misleading meaning and example sentences with translations.

You can study Italian to English when you want to improve recognition, then reverse the direction to practise retrieval. The app adjusts the prompt, answer and explanation to match. Pronunciation is available through on-device speech synthesis, so you can hear each word rather than learning it only as spelling.

The Common tier covers the traps you are most likely to meet first. Medium and Advanced add less obvious words. Partial false friends have their own tier because the meanings overlap in some contexts but separate in others. You can also choose All when you want a mixed session.

Progress is shown by tier, together with accuracy, session history and a daily study streak. Reminders are optional. The intention is to make a small amount of regular practice visible without turning the app into another general language course.

Offline and private

The word data is bundled with False Friends, so Italian quizzes work without an internet connection. The interface is available in English, French, Spanish and Italian.

There is no app account, no sign-up, no tracking and no analytics. Progress can sync privately across your devices with iCloud. False Friends is a one-time purchase: pay once and keep it, with no subscription or in-app purchases.

It is a deliberately focused app for a recurring vocabulary problem. The goal is not to cover all of Italian. It is to make these familiar-looking words feel less familiar in the wrong way.
